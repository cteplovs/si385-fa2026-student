## Data

The records describe 4,424 students who enrolled in undergraduate degrees at the Instituto
Politécnico de Portalegre, in Portugal, between 2008/09 and 2018/19. Each student is labelled with
where they stood at the end of the normal length of their course: graduated, dropped out, or still
enrolled. The data were published by Realinho, Vieira Martins, Machado and Baptista (2022) in the
UCI Machine Learning Repository under CC BY 4.0, so please credit them if you use the data
elsewhere.

The columns fall into groups, as the table below shows.

| Group of columns | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Background | Age at enrolment, gender, marital status, nationality, and whether the student moved to attend |
| Family | Each parent's qualification and occupation, and special educational needs |
| Admission | How and to which course the student applied, their previous qualification, and their admission grade |
| Economy | Portugal's unemployment rate, inflation rate and GDP |
| Money | Whether the student is a debtor, is up to date on tuition fees, and holds a scholarship |
| Semester 1 | Course units enrolled in, evaluated, passed and credited in the first semester, and the grade |
| Semester 2 | The same, for the second semester |

No team will receive the whole file: in class, your team will receive one lens, which is one of the first
four groups of columns paired with one of the last three, for every student, together with the outcome.
Other teams will hold other lenses, and the class will put them together over the module.

## Scenario and question

Portalegre's tutoring team uses a tool built on these records to estimate each student's risk of
dropping out. For this module, suppose the team has asked the class to build the list of students
it contacts, and that it will make the list only once.

> **When should the list be made, and what may it use?**

On Monday your team will choose one answer from a small fixed set, write a short justification,
and reveal at the same moment as every other team.

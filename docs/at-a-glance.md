## Every week

**Before each class**

- Do the reading.
- Take the iRAT on Canvas. It opens **09:50 two days before** class and is due
  **20:59 the night before** --- Sunday for a Monday class, Tuesday for a Wednesday one.
  Five minutes once you open it, one attempt, closed book. *Read first, then open it.*
- The application exercise's dataset and question go up at least two days ahead. Looking
  at them early is optional and often worth it.

**In class (80 minutes)**

| | |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Clarification --- we work through whatever the iRAT showed was broken |
| 0:15–0:23 | Application exercise, **silent**: read the problem, decide what *you* think |
| 0:23–0:50 | Your team argues, then submits |
| 0:50–1:15 | Every team reveals at once; about four are called on to defend |

Your team submits three things before the reveal: **a choice**, **a short written
justification**, and **who was there**. Six points for doing it.

## What you're graded on

| Component | Points | |
|---|---:|---|
| iRATs | 270 | Best 18 of 20 |
| Application exercises | 120 | Best 20 of 23, per team |
| Demonstrations of Understanding | 360 | 3 × 120, individual |
| Team project | 180 | Final report |
| Peer evaluation | 70 | 2 × 35, for completing it |
| **Total** | **1000** | |

**Each DoU** is a marimo notebook (40) plus a **400-word decision memo** (80): the
decision you'd defend, the alternative you rejected, and what you're still unsure about.
Cite the cells and quote the numbers. Over 450 words is returned ungraded.

**Peer evaluation:** you give 30 points across your three teammates and may not split
them evenly. What you *receive* adjusts your share of the project points, and only the
final round counts.

## When something goes wrong

- **You miss a class.** Two RATs and three application exercises are dropped
  automatically. No email, no explanation, no note required.
- **A DoU will be late.** You have three free late days, in whole days, no explanation.
  After that it's 25% per day.
- **Your laptop won't cooperate.** Use [molab](https://molab.marimo.io) --- free, no
  install, runs in a browser. You will never lose an exercise over a broken environment.
- **A quiz question was unfair.** Your team can appeal: one per team, in writing, before
  you leave the room, citing the reading. If it's upheld, everyone on the team gets the
  credit back.
- **Something about how this course runs is a barrier for you.** Come talk to me in the
  first two weeks. Bring the problem even if you don't have a solution in mind.

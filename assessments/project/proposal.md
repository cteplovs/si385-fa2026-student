## The project

The team project is an original investigation of a dataset of your team's choosing,
carried out over the last six weeks of the term. Your team frames a question that the
data can answer, and then works from the raw data to a written conclusion in a single
marimo notebook, using the techniques the course covers along the way.

The project is where those techniques come together. The application exercises and
DoUs ask you to reason about one method at a time on data we chose; the project asks
you to decide which methods your own question needs. A strong project describes the
data honestly before modelling it, with attention to distributions, missing values and
outliers; it uses at least one inferential or model-based analysis, such as a
hypothesis test, a regression, a clustering or a classifier, chosen because it suits
the question rather than because it was covered; and it states plainly what the
analysis cannot support. We hope your
project will also draw on methods from later in the term, such as dimension reduction,
clustering and classification, and we encourage you to plan for them now, even though
the course has not reached them yet.

## Purpose

The proposal lets your team describe what it intends to study before you have spent six
weeks on it, while there is still time to change the dataset or the question. It is not
graded, since a proposal that receives feedback is more useful to you than one that
receives a score, but the teaching team reads every proposal, and each team needs to
submit one before the project work begins.

## Submission

Please submit one marimo notebook, `proposal_<teamname>.py`, of about one page when
rendered. It should run top to bottom from a clean kernel and contain four sections.

### 1. Question

State the question your team is trying to answer, in one or two sentences. A good
question names a population, a measurement and a comparison or relationship, and it
can come out more than one way. "What affects housing prices?" is a topic; "Did
short-term rental density predict rent increases in Ann Arbor neighbourhoods between
2018 and 2024?" is a question.

### 2. Dataset

Name the dataset, say where it comes from, and load it in a cell so that the notebook
shows its shape and the columns you expect to use. The dataset must not be one used in
course materials. If you need to combine sources, say how they join.

### 3. Motivation

Explain in a short paragraph who would care about the answer and what they would do
differently if they knew it. Data about people is encouraged, because that is where the
judgement calls in the analysis tend to be.

### 4. Division of labour

Say what each team member will be responsible for, by name. A useful plan assigns
pieces of the analysis rather than stages of the report, so that every member has
something of their own to defend in the final notebook.

## Assessment

The teaching team reads every proposal and marks it complete or incomplete. A
proposal is complete when all four sections are present and the dataset loads.

If your proposal raises a concern, such as a question the data cannot answer or a
dataset that is too small to support inference, the teaching team will reach out to
schedule a conversation with your team. We will also help you identify a dataset that
may be too large to work with on a laptop, and work with you to scope the project to
the time available.

## After the proposal

The final report (180 points, due December 11) builds on this proposal; its
requirements and rubric are in the syllabus. Your question can change after the
proposal, and often should, but please tell us when it does.
